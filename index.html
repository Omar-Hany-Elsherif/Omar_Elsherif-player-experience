Project 01

When Progress Disappears: Investigating a Mission Save Issue

Scenario: a player of a fictional Assassin's Creed-style game says a completed mission was rolled back.

"I completed the mission last night, but today the game has put me back before it. I'm not replaying all of that again. Can you fix my save?"

Player impact

Hours of effort feel wasted. The player is angry, close to quitting, and worried about trust in the game.

First response

Hi, I'm sorry. Losing progress after a long session is really frustrating, and I understand why you don't want to replay it. I want to look into this properly. Could you tell me: your platform, the game version, the mission name, roughly when you finished it, and whether you saw any error message or a cloud-sync notice? Please do not share passwords or payment details. I will check for known issues and update you as soon as I have something confirmed.

Information needed

Platform, game version, mission name, time played, cloud save on/off, connection state, error text, screenshots if available, whether the game closed unexpectedly.

Check first

Known-issue list, recent update notes, service status, the support guide for save problems. Only approved troubleshooting steps. No promise of recovery until it is confirmed.

Internal case notes: fact versus symptom

Player-reported symptomMission completed, progress rolled back after relaunch.
Verified factOnly what is confirmed: account and platform, game version, known-issue match, logs if the team can access them.
UnknownWhether the save was written, whether sync failed, whether it is one player or many.

Escalate when

The issue repeats after approved steps, several players report the same pattern, there is a clear build or platform link, or save data seems lost. Include the evidence, not just "player is upset".

Follow-up and success

Tell the player what was checked and what happens next, even if the answer is "still investigating". Success is a resolved or clearly explained case, an informed player, and a documented pattern if others are affected.

Skills shown: empathy, careful questions, honesty about unknowns, evidence-based escalation.

Project 03

From Individual Complaints to Actionable Player Insights

Weekly Voice of the Player report for a fictional mobile action-adventure game. Sample: 100 support contacts.

Executive summary: Progression and save concerns are the largest theme (28 of 100) and the most damaging to trust. Crashes come second. The numbers show where to look first. The decision depends on impact and evidence.

Progression / save concerns: 28
Performance / crashes: 22
Unclear mission objectives: 19
Purchase / entitlement confusion: 17
Combat / difficulty feedback: 14
ThemeImpact and signalOwner and next action
Progression / save (28)Very high. Strongly frustrated, uninstall threats.QA + Engineering. Check build and platform pattern.
Crashes (22)High. Often on specific devices.QA. Group by device and OS.
Objectives (19)Medium. Confused rather than angry.Product + Content. Review wording, update FAQ.
Purchases (17)High for trust. Money involved.Support lead + Payments. Verify flow, clear macro.
Combat (14)Low to medium. Opinion-based.Product. Track over time, do not react to one week.

Recommendations

1) Investigate save reports first. 2) Segment crashes by device. 3) Publish a clearer objective FAQ. 4) Review purchase messaging.

Risks and uncertainty

Contacts are not all players. Unhappy players write more. Tagging can be inconsistent. Need app-store reviews, community posts and telemetry before concluding.

Measuring improvement

Contact volume per theme, repeat contacts, resolution time, CSAT, rating trend after fixes.

Project 04

Communicating with Players When Things Go Wrong

A. Negative store review: lost progress

We're sorry about your lost progress. That is frustrating, and we want to help. Please contact Support through Settings > Help with your platform and approximate play time, and we will look into it. Please do not post personal details here.

Why it works: short, calm, public-safe. Needed: platform, time, account via support. Never promise: recovery. Escalate: if more reviews match.

B. Complaint about an update

Thank you for telling us, and we understand the combat change is not what you hoped for. Feedback like yours is shared with the team. We cannot promise a specific change, but your comments are reviewed. If you tell us what felt worse, it will help.

Why it works: no argument, no defense. Never promise: changes or dates. Escalate: if the same feedback grows across channels.

C. Missing purchase and refund demand

Sorry about the problem with your bundle. Please contact Support directly, so we can check the purchase securely. Have your order receipt ready. Do not share card numbers. We will review it under the applicable verification and refund policy and update you.

Why it works: moves money details to a secure channel. Never promise: a refund before verification. Escalate: if the purchase is confirmed but not delivered.

Project 05

A Consistent Workflow from First Contact to Resolution

  1. Acknowledge the player
  2. Understand and categorize the issue
  3. Collect required information
  4. Check known issues and documentation
  5. Investigate or use approved troubleshooting
  6. Escalate with clear evidence if needed
  7. Keep the player informed
  8. Confirm the outcome
  9. Categorize for future analysis

First Response Time

How fast the player hears from us. Fast replies reduce anger.

Resolution Time

How long until solved. Long cases need a check.

CSAT

Player rating of support. Shows quality, not just speed.

SLA Attainment

Share of cases within target times.

Repeat Contact Rate

Players who write again for the same issue. High means unclear answers.

Volume by Category

Which problems are growing.

Escalation Volume

How many cases need other teams. Signals bugs or missing guides.

Knowledge-Base Effectiveness

Whether articles reduce contacts, where measurable.
